# da-training-portfolio
Hi, I’m Sandip Patekar. I’m a data and business intelligence professional with over 14 years of experience turning data into dashboards and reports that business teams use to make decisions. I’ve worked across pharma, medical, and insurance claims, most recently at Sedgwick, supporting claims teams with reporting and data quality.

My toolkit is SQL on Oracle and SQL Server, Qlik Sense and QlikView, SAP BusinessObjects, and Excel. At GlaxoSmithKline, I built Qlik dashboards for sales forecasting. At Cardinal Health, I led BI projects across medical and pharma and built a governance and incident dashboard for the production support team. At Sedgwick, I tuned slow dashboards, validated data, resolved discrepancies, and trained users.

I chose data work because I like the moment a messy set of numbers becomes a clear answer someone can act on. I enjoy the detective side, finding out why two reports disagree, and the communication side, explaining it simply.

To me, a data analyst does four things. They gather and clean data, analyze it to find patterns, present it so non-technical people understand it, and help the business decide what to do next. Accuracy matters most, because a decision is only as good as the data behind it.

I’m excited about this role because it lets me do that work close to the business. Thank you.

Follow-up 1: “Why did you choose a career as a Data Analyst?”

Early in my career I was building QlikView reports for business users, and I noticed that the best part of the job was not the tool. It was watching someone make a decision faster because the numbers were finally clear. That made me want to work with data full time. Over the years I have built on that with SQL, data modeling, and dashboards in pharma, medical, and insurance claims. I stayed in data because the work is always varied and the quality of the answer really matters. Every business has questions it cannot answer without trustworthy data, and I enjoy being the person who finds the answer and explains it in plain language.

Follow-up 2: “Tell me about a time you used data to make a decision” (STAR)

Situation: At GlaxoSmithKline, the sales team needed a clear view of sales forecasts based on the data they provided. The numbers were spread across several sources.
Task: I was asked to build Qlik dashboards that showed the forecast so managers could plan with it.
Action: I wrote SQL and Qlik load scripts to bring the sources together, built the forecast views and KPIs, and checked the figures against the source data before release. I then reviewed the dashboards with users and adjusted them to match how they worked.
Result: Managers could review the forecast in one place and base their planning on the same numbers.
